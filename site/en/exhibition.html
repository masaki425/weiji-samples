Art Obulist 2025 Eiji Watanabe Exhibition “Morning Monsters / W Eiji”Art Obulist 2025 ワタナベエイジ展「Morning Monsters / W Eiji」

Morning monsters, two Eijis. The neuroscientist’s exhibits in the Management Building, the artist’s works in the Rest Building, and the paths of an autumn park joining the two.

Dates
Saturday, November 1 – Sunday, December 7, 2025. The exhibition has ended.
Hours
Thursday to Sunday and national holidays, 10:00–16:00. Closed Monday to Wednesday (open on November 3, 24 and 25)
Venue
Okura Park: Management Building, Rest Building and courtyard (Momoyama-cho, Obu, Aichi)
Admission
Free
Organizer
Art Obulist Executive Committee, City of Obu
Planning / Production
Satsuki Yamamoto + Art Obulist Executive Committee
Committee chair
Eiji Watanabe (artist)
Special thanks
Akiyoshi Kitaoka, Seiji Bon
Theme
“Perception” and “recognition”

The exhibition drew the largest attendance in the history of Art Obulist. The title “Morning Monsters” fuses the names of the artist’s solo exhibition “Morning Star” (2024) and the scientist’s optical illusion “Morning Monster” (2020).

Art Obulist (Art + Obu + List, “a list of art from Obu”) is the collective name for the art projects run by the City of Obu. 2025 marked its tenth anniversary.

Venue会場構成

Two buildings in Okura Park, joined by the real space of the park itself. Visitors set out from the science side, walk through the park, and arrive on the art side.

  1. 1

    Management Building: Eiji Watanabe (neuroscientist). Optical illusions, the question-box corner, 17 handwritten notebooks and the evolution simulator, with a wall diagram of the “stream of evolution” on the back wall.

  2. 2

    The thatched gate, hung with a noren curtain and banners

  3. 3

    The central plaza

  4. 4

    The inner gate. Cut-outs from “Tunnel of Eden” mark the path

  5. 5

    Rest Building: Eiji Watanabe (artist). 24 works, with “Sea of Eden” and “Names of Stars” in the tatami rooms.

  6. 6

    The hut in the courtyard, “mo n do ri – an transformed”: home to the mamedoi pop-up café, Tiny shop the W Eiji and the performances

The real-life circuit in the autumnal Okura Park, from the administration building to the recreation building, leading through a thatched gate, across the central plaza, and through another, smaller gate, was the ultimate alternative space.

Hidehiko Komatsu, “Circuits and Constellations,” catalogue p.53

Crossings二人の交差点

  1. Funhouse

    A hut in which balls roll uphill, built by the artist. The scientist never saw it being built.

    The scientist’s proposal. The mismatch between what the eyes see and what the sense of balance reports makes a physically impossible event seem to happen.

    The only joint work

  2. Toilet paper, left and right

    On the left, the artist’s ⑮ “Toilet Tissue.” A single roll of toilet paper that runs on into the shape of tissue paper is a question of language.

    On the right, “Ebbinghaus Illusion” (the artist’s list of works, ⑯; presented by the scientist). Two rolls whose cores appear to differ in size are a question of perception.

    So the right is science and the left is art. The same material, though.

    Eiji Watanabe (neuroscientist), artist talk, 2025.11.22

    The same material, read two ways. Satsuki Yamamoto saw in this pairing the difference between “what am I looking at?” and “what am I being shown?”, and called it emblematic of the whole exhibition.

  3. Sea of Eden

    Fish cut out of illustrated encyclopedias, spread across a tatami room.

    ⑫ “Sea of Eden, Fish Liberated from Optical Illusion.” Seeing the artist’s “Sea of Eden,” the scientist wondered whether “there is some place where the roots coincide,” and added a new illusion during the exhibition. In the “big car” illusion, perspective changes the apparent size of the cars, while the fish, on a layer of their own, stay the same size.

  4. Butterfly’s-Eye View

    The artist’s work “Butterfly’s-Eye View,” a series of butterflies cut out of illustrated encyclopedias.

    The scientist’s list of works: ⑯ “Tunnel of Eden.” An homage from the scientist to the artist: the butterflies of “Butterfly’s-Eye View” mark the path between the Rest Building and the Management Building.

  5. A colorful world on waking

    The experience the artist had at 28, when he felt that he had “understood.”

    The scientist’s answer to a question from the question box, “What inspired you to do research in this field?”

    When I wake up in the morning and open my eyes, what I see is a magnificent, colorful world. To me, this is a very mysterious thing.

    Eiji Watanabe (neuroscientist), answer from the question box, catalogue p.13

    The two experiences coincided: the words that bound the two together
